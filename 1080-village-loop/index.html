Project Overview

A two-minute read for anyone landing on this project cold.

Stiltz Duo Alta home lift installation in a 2008 Ross Chapin–designed two-story Craftsman cottage. Aging-in-place project for original owners David Albright and Diane Driver, who have lived at 1080 Village Loop since 2010. Hemlock & Hammer is the GC; the Stiltz dealer installs the lift unit and pulls the L&I conveyance permit. H&H scope is structural prep, electrical, finish carpentry, and project management.

01.1 The Project in One Sentence

Cut a 31"×28" hole through a vaulted upper-floor ceiling in a Ross Chapin cottage, frame the rake down level to meet 8' minimum head clearance, run a dedicated 220V/15A circuit, install supporting structure, and finish to match the original architectural detail — so the dealer can install the lift cleanly and a 15-year-tenured couple can stay in their home another 20 years.

01.2 Why This Project Matters

For the Client

Albright and Driver have ~$709K in equity in this home and 15+ years of tenure in a tight-knit pocket neighborhood. This isn't a remodel — it's a stay-put investment in their next chapter. The lift is the single intervention that lets them age in place without losing their home, their community, or the Chapin design they bought into.

For H&H

Tristan has already worked three Highlands homes; this is the fourth. Snowberry Close is a high-pedigree micro-market with eight cottages and a recurring need for sympathetic alterations as original owners age. Doing 1080 right is a referral-engine play, not a one-off.

02.4 Engineering Letter — Required Contents

Tristan must obtain a written letter from Stiltz engineering before bidding. The letter must confirm:

  • Minimum floor-to-ceiling height for the Duo Alta in this configuration (confirm 8'-0" or document any approved variance)
  • Exact ceiling penetration dimensions and structural connection requirements
  • Exact floor opening dimensions and required support detail at the upper landing
  • Lift-footprint placement vs. existing window openings — confirm whether the 31"×28" footprint can sit between or beside the two windows on the rake wall without compromising window headers, jambs, or glazing. The framing-down work to achieve 8' clearance must not foul existing rough openings. Engineer to advise on minimum offset from window jamb to lift envelope.
  • Electrical specification: amperage, wire gauge, breaker, GFCI, outlet placement
  • Phone line vs. cellular emergency communication requirement
  • Confirmation that the dealer pulls the L&I conveyance permit and provides permit number to H&H before the floor is cut
  • Equipment delivery logistics — site delivery vs. dealer staging, ferry implications

03.2 Demolition & Site Protection

  • Floor protection from entry to work zone — Ram Board or equivalent on all hard surfaces, taped seams
  • Plastic dust containment with zipper doors at the work zone perimeter
  • HEPA air scrubber on site during demo and drywall work
  • Demo of upper-floor ceiling drywall in lift footprint plus working perimeter
  • Demo of lower-floor ceiling drywall as required for structural access
  • Remove and store any trim, baseboards, or fixtures in the disturbance zone for reinstall
  • Daily cleanup; debris haul-off included
Highlands Risk Profile Tristan's prior experience: Chapin cottages are "more trim than framing." Carry a meaningful demo contingency for buried surprises (rerouted ducts, plumbing in unexpected places, non-standard framing details). This is by design, not by accident — Chapin's published ethos is "simple materials, rich detail."

03.3 Structural Framing

  • Frame the rake/cathedral ceiling down to a level surface at minimum 8' floor-to-ceiling at the lift footprint, plus working clearance for ceiling penetration
  • Cut floor opening per Stiltz published specification (dealer provides exact dimensions)
  • Install structural header at the opening per stamped engineering detail
  • Stamped detail required from licensed structural engineer for the header and any load-path modifications — separate engineer fee
  • Blocking, ledgers, and any backing required for ceiling finish at the new level surface
  • Floor framing reinforcement if upper-level step-up condition is the result of a non-standard joist layout — assess on demo
  • All framing per IRC 2021 as adopted by Island County

03.4 Electrical (Subcontracted)

  • Dedicated 220V / 15A circuit from main panel to lift location per Stiltz spec
  • Install dedicated outlet at upper or lower landing per dealer's wiring diagram
  • Confirm panel capacity; call out any required panel upgrade as separate line item
  • Coordinate phone line / cellular module for emergency communication
  • Lighting changes at modified ceiling — relocate or replace fixtures in framed-down area
  • All work permitted, performed by licensed electrician, inspected by Island County

03.5 MEP Coordination

  • Survey for any HVAC ductwork, plumbing, or low-voltage in the framing-down zone or floor opening — reroute as required
  • Mechanical sub coordination if ductwork must be relocated
  • Smoke detector relocation per code if ceiling level changes affect placement

03.6 Insulation & Drywall

  • Insulate the new framed-down ceiling to existing R-value or current code minimum, whichever is greater
  • Vapor barrier per WA energy code
  • Drywall hang, tape, mud, sand, prime — match existing finish texture (Highlands homes likely have a specific texture; sample and match)
  • Drywall patches on lower-floor ceiling around opening trim

03.7 Finish Carpentry & Paint

  • Trim restoration around the lift opening at both floors — Stiltz provides standard trim ring; verify if homeowner wants custom trim to match existing house style
  • Reinstall all baseboards, casing, and trim removed during demo
  • Ceiling treatment at framed-down area to match adjacent rooms (flat, knockdown, orange peel — match)
  • Floor patch and refinish around lift opening at upper floor
  • New floor lid trim ring at lower floor (Stiltz standard or custom)
  • Prime and paint all new and patched drywall surfaces
  • Match existing wall and ceiling colors; full-wall repaint if patch areas exceed 30% of any single wall

03.8 Closeout

  • Final cleaning of work zone, debris removal, dust wipe-down beyond the work area
  • Punch list walkthrough with homeowner
  • All warranty docs packaged: Stiltz 24-mo parts, H&H workmanship, electrical sub, structural engineer letter, building permit final, L&I conveyance certificate
  • Photos of all concealed conditions before drywall close-up — provided to homeowner as record set

03.9 Exclusions — State Clearly in the Bid

  • Stiltz Duo Alta lift unit, dealer freight, dealer install labor, dealer service contracts (direct contract: homeowner ↔ dealer)
  • WA L&I conveyance permit fee (paid by Stiltz dealer)
  • Annual Operating Certificate fees post-installation
  • Furniture moving, art removal, or storage of homeowner belongings
  • Structural reinforcement beyond the engineer's stamped detail (treated as change order if discovered during demo)
  • Asbestos or lead paint abatement (not anticipated for 2008 build)
  • Landscape or exterior repair for envelope penetrations (none expected)
  • Smart home / network integration with the lift (Stiltz lift is standalone)

07.3 Likely Lift Footprint Locations

Without floor plans in hand yet, the most probable lift footprint locations in a 1,397 sq ft 2-story Chapin cottage:

  1. Adjacent to existing staircase — minimal floor-plan disruption, but the rake is highest over stair openings (good for clearance).
  2. Corner of main living area cutting up to loft — more disruptive to architectural flow, but may have better head clearance.
  3. Through a closet stack (downstairs closet under upstairs closet) — Chapin's storage-rich design may offer aligned closet locations. Most architecturally sympathetic if floor plan permits.

Confirm at site survey. The Stiltz template (full-size mockup) the dealer brings determines which option works.

07.4 Finish-Quality Bar — Set Expectations Early

  • Trim profiles: Stock big-box profiles will not match. Budget for custom milled trim or careful match-milling of existing profiles.
  • Drywall texture: Confirm at survey — Chapin homes often use smooth-finish drywall, not orange peel or knockdown. Smooth-finish patches are harder to blend invisibly; specialist drywall finisher recommended.
  • Paint match: Homeowners may have original paint records from the builder. Ask Erl Bangston (developer) or Ross Chapin Architects directly if records survive.
  • Hardwood floor patching: 17+ year old hardwood will have patina. Plan for sand-and-refinish of the entire affected room rather than spot-patching.
  • Sustainability cues: Built Green-certified homeowners often want sustainable choices documented (low-VOC paint, FSC trim, etc.). Note as likely homeowner preference.
